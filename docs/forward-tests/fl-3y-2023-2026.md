# FL backtest, three years — 2023-09-25 to 2026-09-25

**Verdict.** Over three years and the same 23 names, the 1-minute rule as traded loses
−7,680R. The 15-minute rule as first tested loses −310R. The candidate from the exit review,
15-minute bars, regular hours, volume above 1.5× average, **0.75×ATR stop moved to breakeven
at +0.5R, hold to close**, is **+169R on 2,963 trades, positive in every full year.** That is a
real, repeatable, thin edge whose paper profit is about the size of realistic friction: two
cents of adverse slippage on every stop fill halves it, five cents erases it, and commissions
take another 48 to 96R. It is worth trading only with excellent stop fills, only in the first
hour, and only on the single stocks; it is not worth trading on the ETFs and commodities.

Data: Massive.com `us_stocks_sip/minute_aggs_v1`, 740 trading days, 23 tickers (three of which
did not exist for part of the span: IBIT from 2024-01, GLND and GRML later; they are skipped as
"no data" until their files begin). Script `backtest/backtest_fl_week.py`, fetched with
`backtest/fetch_flatfiles.sh`. Universe caveat: these 23 names were chosen on 2026-09-24/25;
running them back three years carries selection bias in both directions (they are today's
in-play names, not 2024's).

## Friction: the part that decides whether this is tradeable

Every stop in the table above fills exactly at the stop price. With 2,513 stops in three years,
the assumption is the whole result:

| Adverse slippage per stop fill | R over three years |
|---|---:|
| 0 | +169 |
| $0.02 | +85 |
| $0.05 | **−41** |
| $0.05, with no entries after 15:00 | +73 |
| $0.05, no entries after 15:00, stop 0.5×ATR | +235 |

Commissions: 722,000 shares traded both sides; at $0.005 per share that is $3,610 (48R), at
$0.01 it is $7,220 (96R). Neither is in the P&L above.

**So, honestly:** on paper +169R to +270R over three years, roughly 55 to 90R a year at the
SIM unit, which is $4,000 to $7,000. After two to five cents of stop slippage and commissions,
somewhere between flat and +50R a year. The variant that survives friction best is the
tightest stop with no late entries, which is also the variant most exposed to fill quality.

## Improvements the three-year splits pointed at (2026-09-26, second pass)

David's challenge: $12,688 over three years does not beat the index. Two answers, one about
framing and one about structure.

**Framing.** The dollar figure is an artifact of a fixed $75 risk unit. The candidate's average
position was $11,520 and its peak concurrent exposure $164,383; on that peak it returned 7.7% in
three years while SPY returned 78.7%. On those terms the index wins, and by a lot. The
comparison that matters for a risk-defined strategy is return per unit of drawdown, because R
can be scaled and the index cannot: the candidate made 56R a year against a 42R drawdown, a
ratio of 1.35, daily Sharpe 1.48. That is mediocre, and it is the number the improvements below
move.

**Structure.** Each lever alone, three years, same sizing:

| Lever | Trades | R | Per trade | Read |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Candidate | 2,963 | +169 | 0.057R | |
| No entries after 11:00 | 2,238 | +265 | 0.118R | the edge is the first hour |
| Drop the ETFs and commodities (SPY QQQ IWM XLE SLV IBIT USO) | 1,997 | +231 | 0.115R | tight-stop momentum needs single-stock range |
| Shorts only | 1,493 | +139 | 0.093R | shorts carry 5× the longs, but longs are not negative |
| Volume 2.0× | 1,743 | +234 | 0.134R | |
| Partial profit, half off at +1R | 3,983 | **+25** | | win rate 33%, P&L gone |
| Partial profit, half off at +2R | 3,623 | +95 | | same lesson |

**Win rate cannot be bought.** Taking half off at +1R triples the win rate and removes 85% of
the profit, because the whole return is the tail: runners that go 5 to 8R by the close. Any
mechanism that raises the win rate does it by selling that tail. The right target is per-trade
expectancy and drawdown, not win rate.

**Combined, three years:**

| Configuration | Trades | R | R/yr | Per trade | Max DD | Return/DD | Sharpe | 5¢ slip |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Candidate | 2,963 | +169 | 56 | 0.057 | 41.7R | 1.35 | 1.48 | −41 |
| **+ no entries after 11:00, no ETFs** | 1,597 | **+292** | **97** | **0.183** | **12.7R** | **7.6** | **3.46** | **+198** |
| + no entries after 10:15, no ETFs | 999 | +278 | 93 | 0.279 | 8.8R | 10.6 | 3.97 | |
| + 11:00, no ETFs, shorts only | 744 | +187 | 62 | 0.251 | | | | +145 |
| + 11:00, no ETFs, volume 2.0× | 1,014 | +261 | 87 | 0.257 | | | | +200 |
| + 11:00, no ETFs, stop 0.5×ATR | 1,551 | | | | | | | +301 |

The two filters together cut the trade count by half, keep 90% of the profit, cut the drawdown
by two thirds, and turn the friction column from a loss into +198R. Every year is positive
under them (2023 +7R, 2024 +119R, 2025 +51R, 2026 +115R). Peak concurrent exposure falls to
$72,027, on which +$21,906 is 30% in three years; still under the index on raw capital, but
at 97R a year against a 12.7R drawdown, an operator sizing R at 0.5% of equity would compound
roughly 48% a year with a 6% drawdown, and at 1% roughly double with a 13% drawdown. That is
the comparison the index loses.

**Caveats, in proportion.** Both filters were chosen after looking at the three-year splits,
so this is in-sample selection; the defence is that they are structural (first-hour momentum;
instruments with single-stock range) rather than tuned, and that all four calendar years agree.
The universe is still today's 23 names run backwards. Stop fills are still assumed clean except
in the slippage column. Commissions on the improved set are about 20R.

## What this means for Monday

1. **Do not trade the 1-minute rule or the 15-minute rule with a 1.5×ATR stop.** Three years
   say the same thing the week said.
2. **Run the improved configuration as a 1R experiment:** 15-minute bars, regular hours,
   volume above 1.5× average, 0.75×ATR stop, breakeven at +0.5R, **entries only between 09:45
   and 11:00, single stocks only**, hold to close. Expect about two signals a day across a
   16-name list, four in ten scratched, five losing half a unit, one running.
3. **Measure your stop fills.** The DAS execution log records every fill; the difference
   between the stop price and the fill price on the first twenty stops decides whether this
   rule has an edge in your hands. Two cents is the line.
4. **The 0.5×ATR variant is not a free upgrade.** It is better on paper because its stops are
   closer, which is exactly where slippage bites hardest. Test it after fills are measured.
5. The driver needs `--bar-minutes 15`, `--stop-mult 0.75`, `--be-after 0.5`, `--last-entry
   15:00`, and a breakeven order update through `%OrderAct`. That is the next build.

        --stop-mult 0.75 --size-mult 1.5 --be-after 0.5 --json results/fl-3y-15m-candidate.json
